# Market Sizing & Opportunity

## 1. Define the market precisely
State the product category, customer unit (person, household, SME, enterprise),
geography, currency and year. A vague definition makes every number useless.

## 2. Size it two ways — always
**Top-down:** start from a published total (industry report, official spend
data) and narrow by segment shares. Cite every share.

**Bottom-up:** `# customers in segment × adoption/penetration × purchase frequency × price`.
Build from counts (population, establishments, devices) and observable prices
(competitor price lists). Show the formula with numbers.

- **TAM** — total annual spend on solving this problem in the chosen geography.
- **SAM** — the part your model/channel/segment can actually serve.
- **SOM** — realistic capture in years 3–5. Justify with comparable companies'
  shares, sales capacity, and funnel math — not a flat "1%".

Reconcile: if the two methods differ by >3×, explain why and choose one with
reasons. Present ranges (low/base/high).

## 3. Growth
Historical and forecast CAGR with sources. List 3–5 drivers and 2–3 headwinds
(regulation, tech, demographics, behaviour, macro).

## 4. Market gap
Evidence of unmet need, ranked by strength:
1. Customers already paying for bad workarounds.
2. Repeated complaints in reviews/forums about existing solutions (quote them, cite).
3. Search demand with weak supply (Trends, keyword data).
4. Segments competitors ignore (price tier, geography, language, customer size).
5. Regulatory or tech change that opens a new option.

## 5. Opportunity & timing
Why now, why here, how big the window is, and what would close it.
Score attractiveness 1–5 on: size, growth, gap strength, timing, accessibility.

## Output (`02-market.md`)
Shared handoff format. Required tables: TAM/SAM/SOM (method × low/base/high),
assumptions with sources, drivers/headwinds, gap evidence, attractiveness scorecard.
