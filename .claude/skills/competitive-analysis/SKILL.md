# Competitive Analysis

## 1. Find them
Search: the problem in plain words, the solution category, "alternative to X",
app-store category charts, funding databases (Crunchbase, MAGNiTT, Tracxn), and
the local language. Include:
- **Direct** — same solution, same customer.
- **Indirect** — different solution, same problem.
- **Substitutes / status quo** — spreadsheets, WhatsApp groups, doing nothing, a human service.
- **Global players** that could enter the local market.
- **Dead competitors** — failures teach the most; find why they died.

## 2. Profile the top 5–10
| Name | Type | HQ / markets | Founded | Funding (round, date, source) | Pricing | Customers / traction signal | Strength | Weakness |

Traction signals: app downloads/ratings, web traffic, headcount trend, reviews,
press. Label them as signals, not revenue.

## 3. Compare
- Feature/attribute matrix on what customers actually care about.
- **Positioning map**: two axes that matter to the buyer (e.g. price × convenience). Describe it as a table of coordinates.
- Review mining: top 3 complaints per major competitor (quote + source).

## 4. Conclude
- Barriers to entry (network effects, licences, capital, brand, data).
- **White space** — the uncovered positions on the map.
- Our possible edge and how defensible it is (1–5).
- Threat level of each competitor reacting.
